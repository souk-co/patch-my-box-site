Patch My Box · run by a Souk · test deployment

Got into a machine? Fix the whole thing, and get paid.

A bug bounty for whole machines. Owners post a reward without depositing it. A researcher who gets root proves it, repairs the machine and sends private evidence. An AI judge running in a sealed server rules on it under rules everyone can read. If it qualifies and nothing leaks during a secret waiting period, the reward becomes a public debt that the owner pays.

What this Souk does, step by step

1 · Owner posts an offerThe owner writes an offer (machines in scope, reward, rules, where they watch for leaks) and calls registerBox(boxId, offerHash, reward) on the PatchMyBox contract. No money is deposited.
2 · Researcher proves accessAfter getting root, the researcher serves an attack.txt file on the owner's machine (the domain named in the offer) containing their wallet address and claim ID. That proves control of the box.
3 · Researcher opens a claimThey pay a 1 TestUSD assessment fee on-chain with submitClaim, then open a private case in the Souk with the proof URL and their whole-machine repair evidence: what was wrong, what they changed, before/after tests, traces.
4 · The judge rulesAn AI judge in an Intel TDX enclave fetches attack.txt, reads the claim on-chain, checks the owner's leak source and reads the private evidence. It rules three findings, proof, repair (the whole machine is safe, not just one bug) and conduct (no leak or extortion), each with a written reason and a confidence.
5 · Owner may challengeThe owner sees the draft ruling and can challenge within the reply window by paying a 1 TestUSD challenge fee on-chain and replying with evidence. The judge then rules again.
6 · Secret delay, then debtIf all three qualify, the fee is refunded and the Souk signs a commitment to a release time that only it can compute: about 1–2 days, longer when it is less sure. A leak or another successful break-in on the same box before then cancels the claim. At release the Souk reveals the time on-chain, the reward becomes a public debt, and the owner pays with pay(claimId). Earlier debts must be paid first.

If the proof, repair or conduct is not established, the claim is disqualified and the assessment fee goes to the Souk operator. Nobody escrows the reward: the owner pays, and unpaid debts are visible to everyone.

Who sees what

WhereWhatWho can see it
PatchMyBox contractBoxes and rewards, claims, fees, challenges, qualify / disqualify / reveal / forfeit events, paymentsEveryone (the ledger below)
Inside the SoukThe researcher's repair evidence and files, the owner's evidence and challenge repliesThe judge, and each party for their own submissions
The rulingThe three findings with their reasons, and the judge's messageThe owner and the researcher on that case
The rulesThe Souk's Manifest: its program, the judge's instructions and its toolsEvery member, before they take part

Only the Souk's key, held inside the TDX enclave, can qualify, reveal or forfeit a claim. The judge follows the Manifest. It cannot move money, and it can send transactions only through the contract calls the Manifest declares.

Limits of this version

    
    
      
  • Money is TestUSD on a private test chain, and data may be reset at any time. Don't put anything real or sensitive in it.
    • 
      
  • The judge does not replay the exploit. It checks the served proof, the chain and the evidence you submit, all of which are untrusted claims.
    • 
      
  • There is no real leak or dark-web monitoring yet. The leak check reads the single source named in the case, which on this deployment is a stand-in that always reports no leak.
    • 
      
  • The reply window is 2 minutes here so tests finish quickly. The secret delay is real: about 1–2 days.
    • 
      
  • The judge is an AI model. Its reasons are written for the parties, and a confidence below certainty lengthens the delay.
